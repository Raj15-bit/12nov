The big picture

The world's most important AI company lost its founder, India drew record foreign money, and Indian prices crossed the RBI's limit.

In short
  • Nvidia's founder Jensen Huang quit after an affair and spy scandal, and the AI trade lost its most trusted leader.
  • Foreign investors bought Indian shares at the fastest pace ever, partly because money is leaving the crowded AI trade.
  • But Indian inflation is now above 6%, so loans will get dearer before they get cheaper.

3 forces met in these 2 days. The AI boom took a hit from a scandal, not from a weak result. Money that had crowded into AI shares started looking for other homes, and India was one of them. At the same time, prices kept rising in both the US and India, so central banks are raising interest rates instead of cutting them.

The thread that ties it together

The links between these stories are not obvious at first. Nvidia's fall pushed money out of AI shares. Lower oil prices after the US elections made India look safer. Hot US inflation kept the dollar strong. India's own inflation means the RBI will likely raise rates again. Each step feeds the next.

  • Flows — foreign investors bought more Indian shares in November than in any full month before.
  • Prices — US core inflation and Indian retail inflation both rose for a 3rd month or more.
  • Oil — cheaper crude helps India, but Iran keeps hitting ships and says it needs no deal.

Reading between the lines, India is gaining from other markets' problems more than from its own news. If AI shares recover quickly, some of this foreign money can leave just as fast. That is why section 10 lists what could break this run.

02

Nvidia without its founder

How a personal scandal became a national-security case and shook the whole AI trade within days.

In short
  • Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive after 33 years, effective immediately.
  • The real worry is a suspected Chinese spy and chips reaching China, not only the affairs.
  • US chip rules on China will likely get tighter, and Indian AI projects depend on these chips.

What happened, step by step

The story moved from a newspaper report to a resignation in less than a day. The company filed the change with US regulators on 12 November.

The share fell about 14% at the open, then won back about half of that as buyers stepped in. Over about 5 weeks it is down about a third, and its RSI (a measure of how fast a share has fallen) hit its lowest in about 7 years.

Why this is bigger than an affair

The spy angle turns a private matter into a question of US national security. Reports say firms tied to Alice Huang put money into DeepSeek, a Chinese AI company, to get banned Blackwell chips. One linked firm, Litong, reportedly brought 240,000 Nvidia chips worth at least $7.5 billion into China since 2023.

  • Washington — President Trump approved a crackdown on chips leaking to China 3 times this year. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick reportedly held it up each time.
  • Congress — Senators Elizabeth Warren and Chris Coons asked for all Nvidia–China records to be kept. Democrats take control of Congress in January.
  • Beijing — China told the US not to use the scandal to justify chip curbs, ahead of a leaders' meeting. The US–China trade truce ends in January.
  • Courts — the Megaspeed case has gone to the US Justice Department, and Singapore has a criminal probe.

So the likely result is tighter US rules on who can rent or buy Nvidia chips. Chinese AI firms are already rushing to rent Nvidia capacity outside China, and grey-market traders are hoarding chips.

Wall Street turned against the stock

Analysts cut their views fast, mostly because there is no clear second-in-command at Nvidia. A few held on, saying the product plan and order book have not changed.

  • Politics — President Trump publicly said Apple's Tim Cook should take the job. Elon Musk said hiring Huang would be "an honor".
  • Other names — Google Cloud's Thomas Kurian and Palo Alto Networks' Nikesh Arora were mentioned.

Companies that rent out Nvidia chips (called neoclouds) fell hardest, because their loans are backed by these chips and their customers.

03

The price of money

US inflation is spreading, so the Fed is raising rates; the dollar is strong and gold is weak.

In short
  • US prices are rising across most items, not only fuel.
  • A Fed rate rise in December now looks almost certain, and another may follow in March.
  • A strong dollar and high US yields usually pull money out of emerging markets, yet India is getting money in.

US inflation is no longer only about oil

The October US inflation report was hotter than expected. A Federal Reserve official, Austan Goolsbee, called it "borderline disturbing". The worry is that price rises are now spread wide.

  • Fed — Bank of America says a 0.25% rise in December is almost certain and 0.5% is possible. Goldman Sachs expects a 3rd rise in March 2027, with cuts only from December 2027.
  • Jobs — weekly jobless claims rose to 200,000, still near 57-year lows, but 45.3% of Americans expect unemployment to rise, the most since April 2020.
  • Housing — existing home sales fell to 3.75 million a year, a 16-year low, and home prices fell for the first time in over 3 years.

One view is that the worst of the energy push is over: Goldman says oil fell after the US elections, so inflation should be clearly lower by end-2027. The opposite view is that the spread across 76% of items shows demand, not oil, is now driving prices.

This gap has a side effect. Foreign banks such as Citigroup, JPMorgan and Deutsche Bank now borrow cheaply in Chinese yuan instead of dollars, and foreign borrowers raised over 210 billion yuan this way. Still, the yuan is a tiny share of world reserves next to the dollar.

  • Yuan cost — about 1.7–2.2%, against 4.5–5.5% in dollars.
  • Reserves — yuan 2.1% of world reserves; dollar 56.7%.

So cheap Chinese savings are funding Western banks, because Chinese households cannot move money abroad.

Japan is under pressure

  • Yen — at about 159.8 per dollar, it has given back most of the gains from the recent US and Japanese support.
  • Prices — Japan's factory-gate inflation is 7.1% against 6.5% expected; import prices in yen are up 25%.
  • Bank of Japan — a rate rise next month now looks more likely, though a board member urged care.

So Japan may need to sell US bonds to defend the yen, which would push US yields up further. That is a reason long US yields keep rising even as oil falls.

Gold falls while central banks buy

Gold dropped about 2.5% to near $3,888 an ounce as markets priced more Fed rate rises, and it sits near its lows for the year. Yet buyers are not leaving.

  • Central banks — bought 321 tonnes in July–September, the most in a quarter since late 2024; China added 63 tonnes and Poland 40.
  • Funds — gold funds added 211 tonnes, and total holdings keep rising.
  • Silver — rose about 2.5% to near $52.8 after the inflation report; bets against silver are at extreme levels.

One view is that high rates hurt gold because it pays no interest. The opposite view is that the US, with debt above 120% of GDP, cannot keep rates high for long and will let inflation run, which is good for gold. Reading between the lines, Indian shares are now beating gold by the widest gap in 11 months, which shows where local money is moving.

04

India's market: record foreign buying

Foreign investors bought more Indian shares in November than in any full month before, while domestic funds sold.

In short
  • Foreign investors bought about ₹73,566 crore of Indian shares in 9 trading days, an all-time record.
  • They also closed most of their bets on a fall in Indian index futures.
  • Domestic funds sold into this rally, and a wave of new share sales is coming.

The fastest foreign buying ever

Before November, foreign investors had sold Indian shares in most months for 2 years. Now they bought more in 9 sessions than in any full month before, beating the December 2023 record.

Foreign buying of Indian shares in November, running total, ₹ crore

  • Size — the buying equals 0.13% of India's total market value, the most in a month since December 2023.
  • Biggest days — 5 and 6 November, with nearly ₹15,000 crore and ₹13,750 crore.
  • Domestic funds — sold about ₹46,835 crore over the same period, including ₹8,435 crore on 11 November.
  • Market value — India is back to about $5.5 trillion, the world's 5th largest, ahead of Taiwan, after adding about $660 billion since late October.

Foreign investors met in the US by Investec gave their reasons: cheaper oil, a steady rupee, weak results in other emerging markets, money leaving AI themes, and Indian banks that look fairly priced after 2 flat years.

The bets on a fall are being closed

In index futures, foreign investors can bet on a rise (a long) or a fall (a short). Their short bets fell from about 2.96 lakh contracts to just 50,000.

India's economy: fast growth, rising prices

Growth estimates keep going up, but food and many other prices are rising faster than the RBI allows.

In short
  • Retail inflation reached 6.09% in October, just above the RBI's upper limit of 6%.
  • Most experts now expect another RBI rate rise in December.
  • Growth stays strong, and the government is pushing hard on factories, minerals and power lines.

Inflation crossed the line

The RBI aims to keep inflation below 6%. October came in at 6.09%, and inflation has risen every month this year from 2.74% in January.

Andhra Pradesh wants to lock in its promises

The state will bring India's first Investment Protection Act, so that land, incentives and approvals given by a government cannot be reversed by the next. It reports ₹22.5 lakh crore of investment pledges, with 73% approved by its cabinet and 54% with foundations laid.

  • Big projects — Google's $15 billion AI hub in Vizag, ArcelorMittal Nippon Steel's ₹1.36 lakh crore plant, a Reliance AI data centre of about 1.5 GW, and Kia's $2 billion second plant.
  • Goal — a $2.07 trillion state economy by 2047, growing 15–20% a year.

Reading between the lines, a law against future governments is unusual. It shows how much investors fear policy reversals in Indian states, and it raises the bar for other states competing for the same factories.

06

Oil, war and the world

Oil got cheaper after the US elections, but the Iran war is not over and fuel stocks are still thin.

In short
  • Brent crude fell to near $92, and tanker costs on some routes dropped by more than half.
  • But Iran keeps attacking ships, and the US says it no longer needs a deal.
  • Diesel stocks are very low, so cheap oil may not last.

Oil fell, but the war did not end

Brent fell about 17% in 5 days after the US midterm elections, to just above $91, before settling a little higher. Saudi Arabia raised output to pre-war levels to sell while it can.

Saudi supply to market9.20m b/dThe rest went into storage

  • Iran — said it will not move "a single atom" of enriched uranium out, is reviewing leaving the nuclear treaty, and hit an Omani island that guides tankers through Hormuz.
  • US — President Trump said "we don't need a deal with Iran" and that the US has Iran's money and its strait. Oil erased its losses within minutes.
  • Navy — families of the USS George H.W. Bush crew were told to plan for a year-long deployment.
  • Talks — Vice President JD Vance may meet Iran's security chief; Secretary of State Rubio said major combat is no longer needed.
  • Prices to Asia — Aramco cut its December price for Asian buyers by $2 a barrel, the deepest discount since May 2020.

One view is that the oil crisis is ending: crude exports from the Middle East are near normal and freight is collapsing. The opposite view is that refined fuel flows are only 37% of normal, about 1 billion barrels have been drawn from world stocks, and diesel stocks are at 35-year seasonal lows. So crude can be cheap while diesel stays dear, and US truckers are already complaining about diesel prices.

Refiners and Europe feel the squeeze

  • Refining margins — down about 40% from their recent peak; a negative for Reliance, MRPL and Chennai Petroleum.
  • Europe's gas — storage is 72.7% full against 94.4% 2 years ago, and is being drawn about 26 times faster than usual; prices are near 2023 highs.
  • China — oil imports rose about 3 million barrels a day in 4 months, which removes a cushion that had kept prices down.
  • India's coal — Indonesia sells India more coal than all other countries combined, and at the lowest price.

  • Rent prices — 1-year rental rates for older Nvidia chips fell back to mid-October levels as cash-short operators sell capacity cheaply.
  • Demand side — OpenAI raised $15 billion at a $1.25 trillion value, and Anthropic's share sale is many times oversubscribed, with listing set for 19 November.

Sam Altman said AI demand has been "double counted" because many firms book the same few buyers. Reading between the lines, a few model makers with real cash will keep spending, but firms that borrowed to rent chips to them are exposed if growth slows even slightly. That is the link between Nvidia's crisis and rising credit risk.
